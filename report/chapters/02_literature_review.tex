\section{WiFi Sensing and Channel State Information}

\subsection{The physical basis}

An 802.11n transmitter using a 20\,MHz channel divides the band into 64 OFDM
subcarriers. The receiver estimates the complex channel response on each
subcarrier from the Long Training Field (LTF) at the start of every frame,
producing a vector $\mathbf{H} = [H_1, \dots, H_{64}]$ of complex channel
estimates. Of the 64 bins, the DC bin and the guard-band bins carry no
energy, leaving 52 usable subcarriers at 20\,MHz.

The received channel is the superposition of all propagation paths between
transmitter and receiver:
%
\begin{equation}
  H_k = \sum_{p=1}^{P} a_p \, e^{-j 2\pi f_k \tau_p},
\end{equation}
%
where $a_p$ and $\tau_p$ are the complex attenuation and propagation delay
of path $p$ and $f_k$ is the frequency of subcarrier $k$. Two properties of
this expression matter for sensing. First, paths with different delays
produce different rates of phase rotation across $k$, so the frequency
profile $\{H_k\}$ discriminates between multipath configurations that RSSI
--- which is essentially $\sum_k |H_k|^2$ --- cannot. Second, when a
reflector moves with radial velocity $v$, its path length changes and $H_k$
acquires a time-varying phase; the resulting Doppler shift is proportional
to $v$. Human limb velocities of roughly 0.5--3\,m/s produce Doppler
components well under 60\,Hz at 2.4\,GHz, which is what sets the required
packet rate.

The availability of CSI to software began with the release of a modified
Intel 5300 driver that exposed per-packet channel estimates
\cite{halperin2011tool}. That tool made the entire subsequent literature
possible, and most classic results --- gesture recognition, respiration
monitoring, localisation --- were obtained on that hardware or on Atheros
chipsets with comparable tooling. Yousefi et al.~\cite{yousefi2017survey}
survey the behaviour-recognition branch of this work and establish the
now-standard processing chain: subcarrier selection, denoising,
dimensionality reduction, and classification.

\subsection{Signal processing for CSI HAR}

Raw CSI amplitude is not directly usable. Three problems recur across the
literature and each has a standard remedy that this project adopts.

\emph{Impulsive outliers} arise from automatic gain control steps and
transient interference, and appear as isolated large excursions in the
amplitude series. The Hampel identifier --- a sliding median filter that
replaces samples exceeding a threshold in median-absolute-deviation units
--- is the conventional first stage, chosen because it removes spikes
without the edge smearing a mean filter would introduce.

\emph{Static components and slow drift} dominate the raw amplitude. The
direct path and reflections from stationary furniture contribute a large
constant term that carries no activity information, while thermal drift in
the transmitter and receiver moves the baseline over minutes. Subtracting a
moving mean (equivalently, high-pass filtering below roughly 0.1\,Hz)
removes both and leaves the motion-induced variation.

\emph{Non-uniform sampling.} Packets do not arrive on a perfect grid ---
contention, retransmission, and scheduler jitter perturb arrival times ---
so windows must be resampled onto a uniform grid before any
frequency-domain feature is computed.

The CARM system \cite{wang2017carm} formalised the link between this
processing chain and physics, deriving a model relating CSI variation to
limb speed and demonstrating that the resulting speed profiles are far more
transferable across environments than raw amplitude. Its central lesson ---
that features closer to the underlying motion generalise better than
features closer to the raw channel --- is directly relevant to the
cross-environment gap reported in Chapter~\ref{ch:results}.

\section{Deep Learning for CSI-Based HAR}

The hand-crafted-feature pipeline that dominated early work has largely been
replaced by learned representations. The window tensor --- subcarriers
$\times$ time, optionally with receivers as a third axis --- is naturally
image-like, which makes convolutional networks the obvious first choice, and
its temporal structure invites recurrent or attention-based models.

The SenseFi benchmark \cite{yang2023sensefi} is the most useful reference
point available, because it evaluates a consistent set of model families ---
MLP, CNN, LSTM/BiLSTM, CNN-RNN hybrids, ResNet variants, and transformer
encoders --- across several public CSI datasets under a shared training
protocol. Two of its findings shape this project's experiment ladder.

First, \textbf{simple baselines are competitive.} Across several of SenseFi's
datasets the margin between a well-tuned shallow model and a large deep
model is modest. This motivates the rule adopted here that a classical
SVM/random-forest baseline on hand-crafted features is mandatory, and that a
deep model failing to beat it should be read as evidence of a broken
pipeline rather than of an inadequate architecture.

Second, \textbf{model capacity is not the binding constraint} on
small CSI datasets; data diversity is. Larger architectures overfit the
recording conditions rather than learning the activity. This is why the
models used here are deliberately small --- a four-block CNN of roughly
$2.4\times10^5$ parameters and a chunked CNN-BiLSTM --- and why the project's
effort is weighted toward dataset breadth over architecture search.

\subsection{The evaluation problem}

The most consequential observation in the recent literature is
methodological rather than architectural. The ACM Computing Surveys review
of WiFi sensing \cite{miao2025survey} identifies inconsistent and
optimistic evaluation as a systemic weakness of the field: a large fraction
of reported results use random window splits, in which temporally adjacent
and heavily overlapping windows from the same recording session appear in
both training and test sets.

The consequence is that headline numbers are not comparable across papers
and, more seriously, do not predict deployed behaviour. A model reported at
97\% under a random split may perform near chance on an unseen subject,
because what it learned was the specific multipath signature of one person's
body in one room on one afternoon. Reported ESP32 LSTM results exceeding
93\% under 5-fold cross-validation should be read in this light --- the
regime, not the architecture, explains much of the number.

This project responds by making the three-split protocol
(Section~\ref{sec:evaluation-protocol}) a structural property of the code
rather than a convention: the splitting functions are part of the dataset
module, every results row records which split produced it, and a guard
detects splits whose test set is missing classes present in training and
marks the resulting metrics as uninterpretable.

\section{HAR on ESP32-Class Hardware}

Moving from an Intel 5300 to a microcontroller changes what is achievable.
The ESP32 family exposes CSI through a documented API, at a hardware cost
roughly two orders of magnitude below a laptop NIC with a patched driver,
but with one antenna, a cheaper oscillator, and 8-bit quantised CSI values.

Moshiri et al.~\cite{moshiri2021csihar} published the reference point for
this hardware class: a public seven-activity CSI-HAR dataset collected with
an ESP32, together with a deep-learning classifier. The dataset is
single-receiver, single-environment, and modest in subject count, which is
representative of the field --- and is exactly the limitation a multi-receiver,
multi-subject, multi-session collection addresses.

Strohmayer and Kampel \cite{strohmayer2023wall} demonstrate long-range and
through-wall activity recognition with the ESP32, establishing that the
hardware's sensitivity is not the limiting factor for room-scale sensing.
The more recent ESP-Fi HAR dataset \cite{espfi2026} continues this line with
a larger and more systematically collected corpus.

Two hardware consequences run through all of this work. The
\textbf{single-antenna constraint} makes phase sanitisation impossible and
forces amplitude-only pipelines, as discussed in
Chapter~\ref{ch:introduction}. The \textbf{packet-rate constraint} is
subtler: CSI is only produced when a frame carrying an OFDM training field
is received, so the sensing rate is the rate of suitable incoming frames.
Sustaining 100\,Hz therefore requires deliberately generating downlink
traffic, a practical detail that shapes the acquisition design in
Section~\ref{sec:csi-acquisition}. Espressif's own \texttt{esp-csi}
reference implementation \cite{espcsi} provides the firmware baseline used
here.

\section{Research Gap}

Three gaps follow from the review, and this project addresses each.

\textbf{1. Honest generalisation reporting on low-cost hardware.} Published
ESP32 HAR results are predominantly single-split, and where multiple splits
are reported the cross-subject case is often absent. The gap is not that
cross-subject accuracy is unknown to be lower --- it is that it is rarely
measured and published alongside the headline figure, which prevents readers
from calibrating any of the numbers. This project reports random,
cross-session, and leave-one-subject-out results for every model, and treats
the spread between them as the primary analytical content.

\textbf{2. Multi-receiver spatial diversity, ablated rather than asserted.}
Deployments using several inexpensive receivers to obtain complementary
multipath views are frequently proposed but rarely tested against the
single-receiver alternative on the same data. Because this project's model
takes receivers as input channels, the comparison reduces to retraining with
one, two, or three channels on identical windows, making the receiver-count
claim falsifiable rather than assumed.

\textbf{3. Fall recall as a first-class metric.} Fall detection is the most
frequently cited motivation for WiFi sensing, and the WiFall
system~\cite{wang2017wifall} established the problem's importance early, yet
falls are commonly folded into a multi-class accuracy figure where a class
holding a small share of the samples can be recognised poorly without
materially moving the headline number. This project reports falling recall
separately for every model and every split, class-weights the training loss,
and --- because a fall is a transient event that a majority-vote smoother
would structurally suppress --- routes the fall alert in the real-time system
around the smoother entirely.

A secondary contribution is the artefact itself: a locally collected
multi-receiver ESP32-S3 dataset with per-session metadata, collected under a
documented protocol in a deployment context (Nepali domestic and
institutional rooms, ISP-supplied consumer routers, congested 2.4\,GHz)
under-represented in the existing corpora.
